how it works :: Transfer% and the hometown test_ _□×

Transfer% is the chance a player is on another FBS or FCS roster next season. It's a logistic regression fit on {{ lang.FormatNumber 0 .n }} player-seasons: every 2024 and 2025 FBS and FCS player, followed to the next season's ESPN rosters ({{ .labels }}). Tested out of time (fit on 2024→25, scored on 2025→26), it ranks players with an AUC of {{ $t.auc }} and beats the base rate's log loss by {{ mul $t.skill 100 }}%. The top tenth by Transfer% moved at {{ mul $t.top10 100 }}% against {{ mul $t.rate 100 }}% for everyone, and holds {{ mul $t.top10Share 100 }}% of all transfers. By level: P4 {{ $t.byLevel.P4.auc }}, G5 {{ $t.byLevel.G5.auc }}, FCS {{ $t.byLevel.FCS.auc }} AUC.

Playing time is games with a stat line over his team's games. At a Power 4 school, a third-year-or-older spot player (1-7 games) moves more than a regular. In the G5 and FCS it flips: a regular moves more (a full season against none: odds x{{ lang.FormatNumber 1 (mul (index $or "gp") (index $or "gp_g5")) }} in the G5, x{{ lang.FormatNumber 1 (mul (index $or "gp") .) }} in the FCS), because bigger programs come for him. Adding it lifted out-of-time AUC from .716. Depth-chart rank, snap volume and the young players ahead of him added nothing on top.

What moves it most: third-year players (odds ×{{ printf "%.1f" $or.exp3 }}) and second-years, ranked recruits, a head-coach change (×{{ printf "%.2f" $or.hc }}, averaged over its chance from the team's record), a losing record, and G5 producers (Power 4 programs come calling). Producing P4 players stay. Players likely to be drafted or out of eligibility leave the other way: Draft% sets the floor on leaving, and stay + transfer share the rest.

Hometown. Distance from home matters, on its own and after every other control. Players within 50 miles of home transferred {{ mul (index $h.raw 0).rate 100 }}% of the time; 600–1,200 miles away, {{ mul (index $h.raw 4).rate 100 }}%. At a home-state school: {{ mul $h.inState.in 100 }}% against {{ mul $h.inState.out 100 }}% out of state. Holding the rest fixed, a home-state school 25 miles away keeps a player at {{ mul (index $h.controlled 0).p 100 }}% Transfer% and an out-of-state school 2,000 miles away {{ mul (index $h.controlled 6).p 100 }}% (home-state coefficient {{ index $h.coef.inState 0 }}, se {{ index $h.coef.inState 1 }}). And when they move, they move home: of {{ lang.FormatNumber 0 $h.moves.n }} transfers, {{ mul $h.moves.farCloser 100 }}% of those 500+ miles from home landed closer, and a school within 100 miles of home was picked {{ (index $h.moves.pull 0).ratio }}× as often as a school at random ({{ (index $h.moves.pull 1).ratio }}× at 100–250 miles, {{ (index $h.moves.pull 4).ratio }}× past 1,000). The team builder weights every target by that pull.

The plan. A program can also let a returner walk to free money: when what he'd cost (his xNIL × his chance to return) signs a realistic target worth at least twice his edge over the next man up, for a net gain, he goes on the let walk list (at most two a team, players worth 0.14 WAR or less). Budget = the program's NIL budget (the yNIL it paid this season, which the NIL block anchors to the revenue-share cap plus outside money), up 4% with the cap. First the next recruiting class is held back (what this year's freshmen were paid). Then the roster is kept in order of value per dollar: each returner's edge over the next man up (college WAR a season by FV) against his market price (xNIL) × his chance of coming back. The budget is a soft target, not a cap: programs go over it, and our number is an estimate, so the true budget is taken as within about 25% of it (an assumption; no program's real number is public). Each dollar costs the chance it's past the true budget, so money under the estimate is nearly free and a dollar well past it has to buy its market worth (WAR at the level's going rate). A returner is kept, and a target signed, while his value per market dollar beats that cost; the rest are can't keep at market. What's left under the estimate is portal space, and a plan can run past it for players worth the money. Next season's starters come from 400 simulated offseasons (each player back with his stay chance; a hole goes to the level's typical first backup), measured against the contender bar: the 67th percentile of this season's units at the level. Targets are players elsewhere likely to move and FCS move-ups who would start, priced at the level's going rate for their position and FV. Targets are ranked by what each team can realistically land: need-weighted WAR per dollar × Transfer% × landing odds, the chance he picks this school if he moves.{{ with .landing }} Landing odds come from a destination model fit on {{ lang.FormatNumber 0 .n }} transfers (where each one went, against every other FBS and FCS school): the level step (FCS players move up to the G5 far more than to the Power 4; Power 4 players rarely drop), program talent against his current school's, playing time open at his position, and distance from home. Tested out of time it put the school a player actually picked in its top 10 of {{ .test.schools }} for {{ mul .test.top10 100 }}% of moves (random: {{ printf "%.1f" (div 1000.0 .test.schools) }}%).{{ end }} FCS move-ups sign at 60% of the going rate (an assumption: few programs bid on them), so lower-tier programs lean on them. A target under twice a random school's landing odds (2 in {{ with .landing }}{{ .test.schools }}{{ else }}264{{ end }}) is left off as a long shot: the landing model is weak out of time, so below that line the odds are mostly noise. Tier and role. Landing odds also weigh which way a move goes: a starter or a blue-chip recruit rarely drops a level (in 2025, 4% of recruited Power 4 starters who moved went down, against 53% of unranked Power 4 backups), and a producer moves up. Refit with those terms, the model's out-of-time skill rose from 7.5% to 8.3% and the actual school's top-10 rate from 19.2% to 20.3%. For an FCS player, the chance he moves is his move-up odds (the chance he's on an FBS roster next season, from his FCS production), since Transfer% can't see FCS production. A signing has to add a real share of a contender room at the program's own level, so Group of Five plans go after cheap FCS move-ups the way G5 staffs do. Depth. Each position counts its backups by the snaps their spot on the depth chart played this season (players ranked by snaps; a QB2 plays 9% of snaps, an RB2 31%, a sixth lineman 39%), so a thin room is a need and a backup who'd play real snaps is worth signing. Next season's chart adds the {{ .next | default "next" }} class and the players with no snaps yet, each at his chance of being on the field here (the recruit model), and young players at their projected FV. The market runs like a draft: teams take turns, a target sits on at most a few plans, and each team signs the best value that fits.

Development. Next season is a year on: every returner past his second year gets his position's step at his years in college, measured on {{ with site.Data.cfb_dev_fit }}{{ lang.FormatNumber 0 .n }} FBS players graded in both 2024 and 2025 (their PFN position percentile rose {{ printf "%.0f" (mul 100 (index .byExp "2")) }} points in year two, {{ printf "%.0f" (mul 100 (index .byExp "3")) }} in year three and {{ printf "%.0f" (mul 100 (index .byExp "4")) }} in year four){{ else }}FBS players graded two seasons running{{ end }}, turned into FV at the board's slope of FV on that percentile. First- and second-year players get the recruit model's projection. The steps are scaled so the league's next-season total (every player at his chance to be on an FBS roster, plus the class and redshirts) matches this season's, since the sport's talent pool is level year to year, and each player's FV varies around its projection by the measured spread (breakouts and slumps). Expected adds the usual portal: every mover who'd make a room better, at his chance to move times his chance to pick this school. The class and the redshirts sit on the depth chart at their chance to be on the field, and start only when their projected FV beats the returners'.

Positions. A returner the position-change model flags (the board's could move to: weight at 22 against NFL size bands and how he plays, checked against 2020-26 combines) plays his new position when the two rooms together gain at least a quarter of the level's minimum signing gain, with 2 FV off for his first year there (an assumption). A flagged player elsewhere is also a target for the room he'd move to. The line is set by spot, left tackle to right tackle: the best five of the likely linemen, each where he costs the least to slide. Flipping sides costs a guard 0.5 FV and a tackle 1, a tackle kicking to guard 1.5 (0.5 when the model flags him inside), a guard snapping 2, a center to guard 1, and a guard or center out to tackle 4 to 6 (assumptions, set to how rarely staffs make each move).

Modes: squeeze = a starter the budget can't keep; reload / retool / reinforce = the top, middle and bottom third of programs by the share of this season's on-field value leaving, moved up a step for the third with the most starting spots that have no likely returner (5+ open is at least retool).
