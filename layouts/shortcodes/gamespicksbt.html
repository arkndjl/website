each market's pick: the model's side when it sits X+ points off the line when the board publishes (the history's opening line). X is the smallest gap whose pick is still worth money at the price we bet (Novig, about -103 a side; DraftKings was -110): on the tuning seasons, what the model's side was worth at the close by its gap, a straight line, scaled to half since a live pick is bet after part of the line's move (the odds archive's first week ran at 0.44 of the opening-line value). under Kelly stakes that cut grows the bankroll fastest: walk-forward on every season with opening lines, it grew about twice as fast as the old cut with the best average pick, which threw away every smaller edge that still pays at -103; the market is switched on only where the picks beat the close on the validation season after them (t {{ $d.keepT }}+; CFB, with no separate season, on the tuning one at t 2+), and it is measured, and switched off if it loses to the close, on the test seasons after that, which choose nothing. opening lines the history got wrong (home and away swapped, or a move to the close past 6 robust sd) are left out everywhere. totals (CFB, NBA) are recentred on the market's level: the model minus its mean gap to the line over the slate and the season's last 100 games, so a pick is a view on that game, not on the league's scoring; it held up as well or better at the close, and the overs and unders even out. a result swings about 0.95 units a bet, the line far less, so the close settles a market in far fewer bets.

stakes (Kelly %) the stake on every pick and in the paper book (scripts/units.py), % of bankroll. our expected returns run hot, so each market's is first calibrated: on the held-out picks, what each was worth at the close against what the model expected, every expectation made out of fold (5 blocks of slates). bad opening lines (mirrored spreads, moves past 6 robust sd, about 9 points) are left out. the stake calibrates on the test seasons, the same seasons that veto a market or segment that loses to the close there, so a market's stake and its on/off check share data. every backtest edge is taken at half its value (a live pick is bet after part of the move from the open; NBA a further half for its injury leak), and a pick with no calibrated edge left at the price it gets (a -110 fallback, say) gets no stake and is not listed. one cap, 5% a bet, for the whole book (Kelly here never asks for more than about 4%). the bet is Kelly on that edge, shrunk for how sure it is (u = edge² / (edge² + its variance)), at full Kelly (λ 1) in every market, the multiple with the most held-out log growth (walk-forward, results drawn from the close): 1x beat 0.75x and 0.9x at the backtest's edge and at half of it, and a fraction only wins if the live edge runs under about 0.44 of the backtest's. the edge is calibrated on the gap (model minus line), each market on its own, so a market's segments are one edge priced on one line: NBA totals picks were worth 2.5% at the close at 4.5-5.5 points and 7% at 7.5-10, so a total at the 5.5-point cut gets well under the cap and a 7-point one the 5% cap. picks on one night barely move together (within-slate correlation about 0), so sizing the whole slate jointly gives the same stakes as sizing each pick alone, and a slate cap of 10-25% changes almost nothing; the drawdowns are full Kelly's price (over a long season, the chance of ever falling to 75% of the peak tends to 75%). refit on every --rules run.
